# EU de minimis State aid and the EU SME definition

Verified against EUR-Lex primary texts on 2026-08-14. Every figure below was read from the
official consolidated text of the regulation cited, not from a secondary source.

---

## 1. The general de minimis ceiling — Regulation (EU) 2023/2831

**Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/2831 of 13 December 2023** on the application of Articles 107
and 108 TFEU to de minimis aid.
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202302831
ELI: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2831

### The number

> **Article 3(2):** "The total amount of de minimis aid granted per Member State to a single
> undertaking shall not exceed **EUR 300 000 over any period of 3 years**."

Recital 3 confirms the rationale: the ceiling was raised from the previous EUR 200 000 "to
reflect the inflation that took place since the entry into force of Regulation (EU) No 1407/2013".

### The period is rolling, not fiscal

> **Recital 11:** "The period of 3 years to be taken into account for the purposes of this
> Regulation **should be assessed on a rolling basis**. For each new grant of de minimis aid, the
> total amount of de minimis aid granted in the previous 3 years needs to be taken into account."

This is a change from the predecessor Regulation (EU) No 1407/2013, which used *three fiscal
years*. See "What aggregators get wrong" below.

### Validity

> **Article 8:** "This Regulation shall enter into force on 1 January 2024. It shall apply until
> **31 December 2030**."

Article 7(3): at the end of the period of validity, de minimis aid meeting the conditions "may be
validly granted for a further period of 6 months".

### Other operative points from the same text

- **Per Member State.** The ceiling is national. Aid from Germany and aid from Spain each count
  against a separate EUR 300 000 pot for the same company.
- **Article 3(3):** aid is "deemed granted at the moment that the legal right to receive the aid is
  conferred on the undertaking … irrespective of the date of payment". Sign date, not payment date.
- **Article 3(4):** the ceiling "shall apply irrespective of the form of the de minimis aid or the
  objective pursued by it and irrespective of whether the aid granted by the Member State is
  financed entirely or partly by resources of Union origin." So a nationally-administered grant
  co-financed from ERDF still eats the same EUR 300 000.
- **Article 3(5):** aid is expressed as a **cash grant, gross**, before deduction of tax. Non-grant
  aid counts at its **gross grant equivalent** (GGE).
- **Article 3(7):** if a new award *would* breach the ceiling, "that new aid shall not benefit from
  this Regulation" — the whole new award falls out, it is not merely trimmed to the headroom.
- **Article 3(8)–(9):** mergers and acquisitions aggregate prior de minimis aid of all merging
  undertakings; on a split, the aid follows the activity it was used for, or is allocated pro rata
  on book value of equity.
- **Article 4:** only "transparent" aid qualifies (GGE calculable ex ante). Safe harbours include a
  loan of EUR 1 500 000 over 5 years or EUR 750 000 over 10 years with ≥50% collateral, and a
  guarantee of EUR 2 250 000 over 5 years or EUR 1 125 000 over 10 years capped at 80% of the loan.
- **Article 5(3):** de minimis "shall not be cumulated with State aid in relation to the same
  eligible costs … if such cumulation would exceed the highest relevant aid intensity or aid amount"
  under a block exemption or Commission decision. De minimis *not* attributable to specific eligible
  costs may be cumulated with GBER aid.
- **Article 1:** excluded sectors — primary production of fishery/aquaculture products; primary
  agricultural production; certain processing/marketing of those products; export-related aid; and
  aid contingent on using domestic over imported goods.

### Central register and the self-declaration obligation

> **Article 6(1):** "Member States shall ensure that, **from 1 January 2026**, information on de
> minimis aid granted is registered in a central register at national or Union level."

Registration within 20 working days of grant (Art. 6(2)); records kept 10 years (Art. 6(3));
aggregated data to the Commission by 30 June each year, first covering 2026 (Art. 6(6)).

The applicant-facing declaration duty is transitional and sits in **Article 7(4)**:

> "Until the central register is set up and covers a period of 3 years, where a Member State intends
> to grant de minimis aid to an undertaking … that Member State shall inform the undertaking in
> written or electronic form of the amount of the aid expressed as a gross grant equivalent and its
> de minimis character, referring directly to this Regulation. … **Before granting the aid, the
> Member State shall obtain a declaration from the undertaking concerned, in written or electronic
> form, about any other de minimis aid received to which this Regulation or other de minimis
> regulations apply over any period of 3 years.**"

Practical reading: the self-declaration remains a live obligation for applicants for years yet.
The register started 1 January 2026 and must *cover* a rolling three-year period before the
declaration duty falls away — i.e. not before 1 January 2029, and later in Member States that
started late.

## 2. "Single undertaking" — the aggregation rule that catches groups

**Article 2(2) of Regulation (EU) 2023/2831:**

> "'Single undertaking' means, for the purposes of this Regulation, all enterprises having at least
> one of the following relationships with each other:
> (a) one enterprise has a majority of the shareholders' or members' voting rights in another
> enterprise;
> (b) one enterprise has the right to appoint or remove a majority of the members of the
> administrative, management or supervisory body of another enterprise;
> (c) one enterprise has the right to exercise a dominant influence over another enterprise pursuant
> to a contract entered into with that enterprise or pursuant to a provision in its memorandum or
> articles of association;
> (d) one enterprise, which is a shareholder in or member of another enterprise, controls alone,
> pursuant to an agreement with other shareholders in or members of that enterprise, a majority of
> shareholders' or members' voting rights in that enterprise.
> **Enterprises having any of the relationships referred to in points (a) to (d) through one or more
> other enterprises shall also be considered to be a single undertaking.**"

Recital 4 adds the case-law layer: the Court of Justice "has ruled that all entities that are
controlled (on a legal or on a de facto basis) by the same entity are to be considered as a single
undertaking", and a holding entity that "actually exercises that control by involving itself
directly or indirectly in the management" is itself part of the economic activity.

**Critical distinction.** "Single undertaking" (de minimis) is *narrower* than the SME definition's
group test:

| | de minimis "single undertaking" (Reg. 2023/2831 Art. 2(2)) | SME definition (Rec. 2003/361/EC Annex Art. 3) |
|---|---|---|
| Control-based links | Yes — the four (a)–(d) relationships, and indirect chains | Yes — the same four relationships, called **linked enterprises** |
| 25%–50% shareholdings | **Not counted.** There is no partner-enterprise concept in the de minimis rule | **Counted pro rata** as **partner enterprises** (≥25% of capital or voting rights) |
| Links through natural persons | Not in the text of Art. 2(2) | Yes, if the enterprises operate in the same or adjacent markets |

So an investor holding 30% of two portfolio companies does **not** merge their de minimis pots, but
does affect both companies' SME headcount and financial data. Getting these two tests confused is
the single most common error in grant-aggregator content.

## 3. SGEI de minimis — the separate, higher ceiling

**Commission Regulation (EU) 2023/2832 of 13 December 2023** on de minimis aid granted to
undertakings providing services of general economic interest (SGEI).
Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202302832
ELI: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2832

> **Article 3(2):** "The total amount of de minimis aid granted per Member State to a single
> undertaking providing services of general economic interest shall not exceed **EUR 750 000 over any
> period of 3 years**."

Same validity window: in force 1 January 2024, applies until 31 December 2030.

Two things to note:

1. **It only covers aid for the provision of the SGEI.** It is not a general uplift for any company
   that happens to do something public-spirited. The undertaking must have been entrusted with a
   service of general economic interest.
2. **Its "single undertaking" definition has a carve-out the general regulation lacks.** Article 2(2)
   of 2023/2832 ends: "However, enterprises performing services of general economic interest that
   have no relationship with each other, except for the fact that each of them has a direct link to
   the same public body or bodies, or to the same non-profit entity or entities, shall not be treated
   as a single undertaking for the purposes of this Regulation."

**Cumulation between the two.** Article 5(1) of Regulation (EU) 2023/2831 provides that general de
minimis aid "may be cumulated with de minimis aid granted in accordance with Commission Regulation
(EU) 2023/2832". The practical effect is that a company holding an SGEI entrustment can hold up to
EUR 750 000 of SGEI de minimis and, separately, general de minimis, subject to the SGEI ceiling
being the overall cap for SGEI-related aid. Where the exact interaction matters to a client, read
Article 5 of both regulations rather than relying on a summary.

**Other de minimis regulations that exist and have their own, much lower ceilings** (relevant if a
company touches farming or fishing):
- Agriculture: Regulation (EU) No 1408/2013 as amended (primary agricultural production).
- Fishery and aquaculture: Regulation (EU) No 717/2014 as amended.
Both are cross-referenced in Article 5(2) of Regulation (EU) 2023/2831. Their ceilings were **not**
verified here and must be read from the current consolidated texts before being quoted.

## 4. The EU SME definition — Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC

Official text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32003H0361
(Annex, Title I, Article 2 — "Staff headcount and financial ceilings determining enterprise
categories")

| Category | Headcount (AWU) | Annual turnover | **and/or** annual balance sheet total |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Medium-sized** (the SME ceiling) | fewer than **250** | ≤ **EUR 50 million** | ≤ **EUR 43 million** |
| **Small** | fewer than **50** | ≤ **EUR 10 million** | ≤ **EUR 10 million** |
| **Micro** | fewer than **10** | ≤ **EUR 2 million** | ≤ **EUR 2 million** |

Verbatim, Annex Article 2:

> "1. The category of micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) is made up of enterprises
> which employ fewer than 250 persons and which have an annual turnover not exceeding EUR 50 million,
> **and/or** an annual balance sheet total not exceeding EUR 43 million.
> 2. Within the SME category, a small enterprise is defined as an enterprise which employs fewer than
> 50 persons and whose annual turnover and/or annual balance sheet total does not exceed EUR 10
> million.
> 3. Within the SME category, a microenterprise is defined as an enterprise which employs fewer than
> 10 persons and whose annual turnover and/or annual balance sheet total does not exceed EUR 2
> million."

### The mechanics that trip people up

- **Headcount is a hard gate; the financial test is a choice.** The headcount ceiling must always be
  met. Of the two financial ceilings, only **one** must be met — turnover **or** balance sheet total.
  A company can blow through the EUR 50 million turnover ceiling and still be an SME if its balance
  sheet total is under EUR 43 million.
- **Headcount is in annual work units (AWU)** (Annex Art. 5): full-time equivalents over the
  reference year, counting employees, subordinated persons deemed employees under national law,
  owner-managers, and partners engaging in regular activity. **Apprentices and students on
  vocational training contracts are excluded**, and **maternity or parental leave is not counted**.
- **Autonomous / partner / linked** (Annex Art. 3). Partner = an upstream enterprise holds **25% or
  more** of capital or voting rights; partner data is aggregated **proportionally**. Linked = the
  four control relationships; linked data is aggregated at **100%**.
- **The investor exemption.** An enterprise can still be autonomous despite a ≥25% holding by
  certain investors, provided they are not linked: public investment corporations, venture capital
  companies, **business angels** ("provided the total investment of those business angels in the
  same enterprise is less than **EUR 1 250 000**"), universities or non-profit research centres,
  institutional investors including regional development funds, and autonomous local authorities
  with an annual budget under EUR 10 million and fewer than 5 000 inhabitants.
- **Public ownership kills SME status.** Annex Art. 3(4): "an enterprise cannot be considered an SME
  if **25% or more** of the capital or voting rights are directly or indirectly controlled, jointly
  or individually, by one or more public bodies" (except for the investors listed above).
- **The two-year rule.** Annex Art. 4(2): exceeding or falling below the ceilings "will not result in
  the loss or acquisition of the status … unless those ceilings are exceeded over **two consecutive
  accounting periods**". Newly established enterprises without approved accounts use "a bona fide
  estimate made in the course of the financial year" (Art. 4(3)).
- **Self-declaration is explicitly allowed.** Annex Art. 3(5): "Enterprises may make a declaration of
  status as an autonomous enterprise, partner enterprise or linked enterprise, including the data
  regarding the ceilings set out in Article 2" — "without prejudice to the checks and investigations
  provided for by national or Community rules."
- Article 2 of the Recommendation itself: the ceilings "are to be regarded as maximum values. Member
  States, the EIB and the EIF may fix lower ceilings."

### The new "small mid-cap" category (2025) — adjacent, not a change to the SME thresholds

**Commission Recommendation (EU) 2025/1099 of 21 May 2025** introduced a definition of *small
mid-cap enterprises* as part of the Omnibus IV simplification package.
https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AL_202501099

This creates a **new, separate category above the SME band**. It does **not** amend the SME
headcount or financial thresholds in Recommendation 2003/361/EC, which remain as tabulated above.
The exact small mid-cap thresholds were **not** read from the primary text during this research and
are therefore deliberately not quoted here — read Recommendation (EU) 2025/1099 before citing a
number. Note separately that several EU programmes use their own operational mid-cap cut-offs — the
EIC Accelerator says "Small mid-caps (up to 499 employees)" and EIC STEP Scale Up says "fewer than
500 employees" — and those are programme rules, not the legal definition.

## 5. What a grants aggregator would get wrong

1. **Quoting EUR 200 000.** That was Regulation (EU) No 1407/2013, which expired 31 December 2023.
   The figure has been **EUR 300 000** since 1 January 2024.
2. **Saying "three fiscal years".** Regulation 2023/2831 uses "any period of 3 years" on a **rolling**
   basis (Recital 11). The old fiscal-year framing produced materially different headroom
   calculations and is now simply wrong.
3. **Repeating the EUR 100 000 road freight transport sub-ceiling.** It existed in Regulation (EU)
   No 1407/2013 (Art. 3(2), second subparagraph). **It does not appear anywhere in Regulation (EU)
   2023/2831** — the word "road freight" is absent from the text. Road freight operators are now on
   the standard EUR 300 000. The prohibition on aid for acquisition of road freight vehicles is also
   gone.
4. **Applying the EU SME group test to de minimis.** "Single undertaking" ignores partner enterprises
   entirely; a 30% investor stake does not merge de minimis pots. Conversely, applying the de minimis
   test to SME status will wrongly classify VC-backed companies as autonomous.
5. **Treating de minimis as an EU-wide pot.** The ceiling is "per Member State". Multi-country groups
   have multiple ceilings; the practical constraint is per Member State per single undertaking.
6. **Telling founders that Horizon Europe / EIC / Innovation Fund grants consume de minimis
   headroom.** They are **directly managed EU funds**, not Member State resources, and are generally
   not State aid at all — so they do not count against the EUR 300 000. Confusingly, Article 3(4)
   *does* pull in aid "financed entirely or partly by resources of Union origin" — but that covers
   funds channelled through and controlled by a Member State (ERDF, ESF+, national RRF measures), not
   grants awarded directly by the Commission or its executive agencies. Get this backwards in either
   direction and the headroom figure is wrong.
7. **Assuming the self-declaration has been abolished.** The central register duty began 1 January
   2026, but Article 7(4) keeps the applicant declaration alive "until the central register is set up
   **and covers a period of 3 years**" — realistically 2029 at the earliest.
8. **Applying the aid at payment date.** Article 3(3) fixes the grant date as when the legal right is
   conferred. A grant agreement signed in December 2026 counts in the 3-year window from December
   2026, even if the money lands in 2027.
9. **Assuming a breach is trimmed.** Article 3(7): the new aid simply cannot benefit from the
   regulation at all. It is a cliff edge, not a taper.
10. **Quoting a single "InvestEU grant".** InvestEU delivers guarantees and investment through
    intermediaries. Any aid element reaching an SME on below-market terms is granted by the
    intermediary under national rules and may itself be de minimis — the certificate comes from the
    intermediary, not from Brussels.

## 6. Could not verify

- The exact small mid-cap thresholds in Commission Recommendation (EU) 2025/1099 (headcount and
  financial ceilings). The recommendation's existence, date and OJ reference are confirmed; the
  numbers are not, and are deliberately omitted rather than guessed.
- The current ceilings in the agricultural (Reg. (EU) No 1408/2013) and fishery (Reg. (EU) No
  717/2014) de minimis regulations, both of which have been amended since adoption.
- Whether any Member State's national central register was live and covering a full three years as
  at the verification date; this determines locally whether the Article 7(4) declaration is still
  required.
